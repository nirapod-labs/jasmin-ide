# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about the code, not the person who wrote it. Assume
the other person is trying to help.

## What is expected

- **Be direct and be kind.** Say what is wrong with a change and why, and say
  it about the change. A question that seems obvious is still worth answering.
- **Take review as help.** A reviewer who finds a real problem has done you a
  favour. A reviewer who is wrong is still worth a plain answer.
- **Give review as help.** Say what you would change and what you would keep.
  Point at the line, not at the author.
- **Accept that people differ.** Experience, language, time zone, health and
  what someone has going on at home are all invisible. None of them is a fair
  target.
- **Respect the process.** A decision that took a discussion is not reopened
  because one person did not like it.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, personal attacks, and remarks about someone's ability, appearance,
  background or identity.
- Harassment of any kind, in the repository, in reviews or in private messages
  that come out of work here.
- Sexual attention or imagery, in any project space.
- Publishing someone's private information without their clear permission.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, or repeating an argument that has been
  settled.
- Anything that would make a reasonable person not want to take part.

## Where this applies

Everywhere this project happens: issues, pull requests, reviews, commit
messages, documentation, and any channel where someone is speaking for the
project.

## Reporting a problem

The repository is private until the first public release, and there is no
conduct contact published yet. The contact is published with the first public
release, in this file. Until then, raise a conduct problem directly with the
person who maintains the repository.

Report what happened, where, and who was involved. A report is read by the
maintainer and is not shared more widely than it needs to be.

## What happens next

The maintainer decides what response fits. Depending on what happened, that
may be a private word, a public correction, a request to step back from a
thread, or a ban from the project spaces. The person who reported it is told
what was decided. A reporter is not expected to keep working alongside the
person they reported while a decision is open.

A maintainer who is the subject of a report does not decide it.
